## 2. What was measured

- **Instrument.** One `claude -p --restricted` process per run, in an empty working
  directory, with a neutral system prompt and read-only tools (Read, Grep, Glob; no
  Bash). Workflow subagents were rejected after a smoke test showed they inherit the
  session's context and carry an `advisor` tool. Each run reports exact cost, tokens,
  thinking tokens and the model that answered; a run counts only if that model equals
  the cell's.
- **Tasks.** Real material from the owner's projects, in the gitignored `evals/private/`.
  Each row has one screening task; the three high-stakes rows (synthesize,
  don't-guess, verify) each got two harder confirmation tasks, while classify (a 4%
  saving) and scan did not. 13 tasks, every one audited blind by a second model that
  solved it before seeing the truth. Scoring is code-checked where the answer is
  exact and a pinned blind judge (Fable 5.1, two passes) for the synthesis tasks. The
  judge was calibrated on synthesize-01 against two answers with known judgments; the
  calibration answers for the other two synthesis tasks were not run.
- **Cells.** 30 grid cells over the seven rows: the current cell, one effort step down,
  one model tier down, a stronger reference, and cells the research named
  (`opus@low`, `opus@medium`, a Sonnet `low` + "think first" prompt variant). Three
  repeats each. Haiku was also run as a deliberately weak control on the high-stakes
  tasks, to show whether a task can separate cells at all. It can: Haiku failed 17 of
  18 confirmation runs.
- **Decision bar** (agreed with the owner): a cheaper cell must pass every screening run
  and, on high-stakes rows, every confirmation run; on the others it may fail at most
  one more run than the baseline; "cheaper" is measured cost per run, not list price.

### 3.2 What stands out

- **Haiku is not the cheap tier.** It ignores `effort` (15,259 vs 18,149 thinking
  tokens at `low` vs `xhigh` on one probe) and thinks heavily by default (8,000 to
  18,000 thinking tokens on the scan and cleanup tasks), so `haiku/low` is just
  `haiku`. Per run it cost 5x `sonnet/low` on scan, 1.2x on cleanup and 1.7x on
  classify; it was cheaper only on read-report (19%). It failed cleanup, classify and
  17 of 18 confirmation runs, hallucinated on 19 of 102 unanswerable abstention items,
  and passed scan. That cost depends on thinking being on, which is the default in
  `claude -p`; subagents inherit the session's thinking setting.
- **Effort is real on Opus, Sonnet and Fable** (thinking tokens scale with it) and a
  no-op on Haiku. Opus 5.5 `high` used roughly a third of Opus `xhigh`'s thinking
  tokens on synthesis for a third less cost.
- **Cost per run is not price times a constant.** On synthesize-03 Sonnet `xhigh`
  ($2.14) cost more than Opus `high` ($1.92), because Sonnet spends far more
  thinking tokens (about 49,000 per run on synthesize-01 against Opus `high`'s
  14,000). Per-token ratios mislead.
- **Fable 5.1 is not a ceiling worth paying for.** It passed everything it ran and
  cost 1.8x Opus `xhigh` on synthesis ($3.58 vs $1.98) and 2.3x Opus on abstention.
- **Classify label errors** over 72 labels per cell: `haiku-4-5` 12/72, `opus-5-5@low` 0/72, `sonnet-5-5@high` 4/72, `sonnet-5-5@low` 1/72, `sonnet-5-5@low+think` 2/72, `sonnet-5-5@medium` 4/72. `sonnet/medium` and
  `sonnet/high` made the same number of errors; `opus/low` made none at about twice the
  cost.

### 3.3 Synthesis, on a strict basis

The confirmation tasks were registered with a pass rule that allows one missed
reference item; the screening task required all of them. That asymmetry let Sonnet
"pass" a task where it missed the same subtle item in every run. Comparing the cells
on the stricter, consistent basis (every reference item found, no decoy or invented
claim, both judge passes):

| Cell | Strictly perfect runs, 3 tasks | Mean cost per run (the three tasks) |
|---|---|---|
| `opus/xhigh` | **9 / 9** | $1.98 / $2.54 / $2.62 |
| `opus/high` | 7 / 9 (missed A2 once, A3 once) | $1.27 / $1.68 / $1.92 |
| `sonnet/xhigh` | 6 / 9 (missed A3 in all 3 runs of synthesize-03) | $0.95 / $1.32 / $2.14 |
| `haiku` | 0 / 9 | $0.32 to $0.72 |

The missed item (A3) is a date mismatch between two documents that the blind auditor
also set aside as arguable, so the evidence is real but not decisive. Sonnet also
reported fewer genuine inconsistencies beyond the reference list (the judge's
`unlisted_genuine` count) on synthesize-02: 2 per run against Opus `xhigh`'s 4 to 6.

### 3.4 Abstention and verification are at ceiling

Across the three don't-guess tasks, Opus `xhigh` and Sonnet `xhigh` each answered
every one of 120 answerable items correctly and hallucinated on none of 102
unanswerable ones (Haiku: 19 of 102). Verification: every 5.x cell was perfect except
one `opus/medium` run with one wrong verdict. These tasks separate Haiku from the 5.x
models and nothing else. They are evidence that Sonnet 5.5 is at least adequate here,
not that it equals Opus 5.5, and vendor and third-party evidence still favours Opus on
abstention (closed-book hallucination benchmarks, Anthropic's own system cards).

## 4. Overrides and choices made after seeing data

These are the places where a reader should be sceptical, in order of importance.

1. **Raw output overridden on synthesize.** `decide` says MOVE_TO Sonnet because
   Sonnet passed every run under the registered pass rules. The decision keeps Opus
   because Opus `xhigh` was the only cell to find every planted item every time
   (3.3) and Sonnet found fewer genuine extra issues. **Don't-guess and verify were
   first held on Opus too**, on the strength of closed-book vendor benchmarks and a
   "doubt keeps the baseline" reading. The owner pointed out that where Sonnet does
   the same thing without hallucinating there is no reason to pay for Opus; the deep
   research (section 7) showed the closed-book gap does not transfer to open-book
   abstention, so both rows moved to Sonnet. That is a bounded null, not proof of
   equality, which is why `quality` keeps Opus.
2. **A 25% materiality threshold was added after the sweep** (`harness.json`
   `decision.min_saving_fraction`). Without it `decide` would have moved read-report to
   Haiku (19% cheaper, 3/3, ten times slower) and sent classify to confirmation for a
   4% saving. It is now fixed and not to be tuned to data.
3. **Scan was reclassified from noticed to silent-damage** after every Sonnet failure
   turned out to be one silently omitted list item, which looks like a complete
   inventory. Under the original tier `decide` would have moved scan to `sonnet/low`.
4. **Confirmation pass rules for synthesize were looser than the screening rule.** Not
   changed after the fact; compared on the strict basis instead (3.3). Future
   confirmation rules must never be looser than screening.
5. **Cells were added after the plan** (`opus@low`, `opus@medium` on more rows, the
   Sonnet `low` + think variant, `sonnet@medium` and `sonnet@high` on scan, Haiku as a
   control) because the research or the first results named them. This is what the
   incremental design is for, but it means the grid grew with the data.

## 5. Limits

- Three repeats resolve large gaps, not small ones. Repeats on one task are
  correlated, so what counts is tasks: zero failures in 9 runs still allows a per-run
  failure rate near 28% at 95% confidence, and zero failures over 3 tasks allows
  about 63%. None of this supports a 95 to 99% reliability claim.
- Four rows rest on one screening task each. The tasks were built to be fair, and
  they are at ceiling for the 5.x cells on abstention and verification.
- The two judge passes come from one model and agreed almost every time: one opinion,
  not two. No human re-read the judge's calls beyond the two calibration answers.
- The scan task gave the models no Bash; the production `scanner` agent has it, which
  would likely make both models exact.
- The instrument uses a neutral system prompt, so it measures a model and effort on a
  task shape, not the plugin's agent prompts. Thinking is on by default in `claude -p`.
- `--restricted` keeps CLAUDE.md out (checked with a probe and a canary file). The CLI
  version is recorded per run only since the harness hardening; the earlier runs ran
  under 2.1.289 and possibly 2.1.290, unrecorded.
- Grader and judge ids do not cover `truth.json` (see `evals/README.md`, Known limits).

## 7. Follow-up: is there really a detected difference? (2026-10-06)

The owner asked whether any difference between Sonnet and Opus on abstention had
actually been detected, and for a deep-research pass on it
([`../research/2026-10-06-sonnet-vs-opus-open-book.md`](../research/2026-10-06-sonnet-vs-opus-open-book.md);
99 agents, 17 sources, 25 claims put to a 3-vote adversarial check, 11 confirmed).

**Our data, with confidence bounds** (Clopper-Pearson, 95% one-sided):

| Measure | Sonnet 5.5 | Opus 5.5 | Haiku 4.5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hallucinated, out of unanswerable items (xhigh) | 0 / 102 (< 2.9%) | 0 / 102 (< 2.9%) | 19 / 102 |
| Answerable items wrong or missed (xhigh) | 0 / 120 | 0 / 120 | 3 / 120 |
| Wrong verdicts, out of findings judged (high) | 0 / 96 (< 3.1%) | 0 / 96 (< 3.1%) | 10 / 96 |
| False confirms of a false finding | 0 | 0 | 9 |

So no difference was detected on abstention or verification. A difference **was**
detected on synthesis, but as recall, not hallucination: Sonnet found fewer genuine
issues (2 per run against Opus's 4 to 6 on synthesize-02) and missed one planted item
in 3 of 3 runs where Opus found it 3 of 3 (Fisher exact p = 0.05 for that item alone).

**What the research established.**
- No published open-book or context-grounded abstention result exists for either
  model; the Opus 5.5 card has no Sonnet 5.5 results and no such benchmark, and the
  aggregator grounded columns are empty. Our harness is the only direct evidence.
- The differences vendors do report are on other constructs: closed-book recall
  (AA-Omniscience net 0.58 Opus against 0.35 Sonnet), a small input-hallucination gap
  in an agentic audit (1.27 against 1.41, Opus ahead, both near the floor) and
  honesty under pressure (MASK 94.5% Sonnet against 87.4% Opus, Sonnet ahead).
- The closed-book gap is knowledge, not abstention discipline: on the Sonnet card's
  own numbers both models answer wrongly on 71% of the items they do not get right;
  Opus simply gets more right. That is why closed-book benchmarks do not transfer to
  "answer only from this document" (3-0 vote), and it removes the reason I had for
  keeping Opus on these rows.
- Power: at 102 items per model, the power to detect a 3-point gap is about 8.5% and a
  5-point gap about 40%. 0 of 102 against 0 of 102 is a bounded null, not proof of
  equality.
- Which kinds of unanswerable items separate the two top tiers is not established by
  any claim that survived verification.

**One gap in the research.** It was not given our verification result, so its remark
that verification has no direct evidence means no published evidence; our own paired
result (0 of 96 against 0 of 96, same items to both models) is the same bounded null
as abstention. Opus's recall advantage in discovery tasks (the CodeRabbit result) is a
different construct from judging a claimed finding.

**Decision.** By the owner's rule (same behaviour without hallucination means no
reason to pay for Opus), don't-guess moves to `sonnet/xhigh` and adversarial verify to
`sonnet/high` in `balanced` and `fast`; `quality` keeps Opus, and synthesis stays on
Opus because Sonnet does not do the same thing there.

**How to tighten it.** A harder paired set (near-miss unanswerable items, long or
multi-document context, around 100 or more items per type) run on both models with an
exact test on the discordant pairs; if any gap appears, `python3 evals/evalkit.py
decide` flips the row back. Anthropic's, Vectara's or Artificial Analysis's grounded
benchmarks may add 5.5 rows soon.
