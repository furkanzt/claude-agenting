   Opus and Sonnet agents, as the rules say, but its Opus agents called the session's advisor, Fable 5.2, 19

   only help Fable, and Fable did not win. For the UI row the rules stood as written: both designers got the same

2. **Most pitfalls were first noticed by AI review passes**, some of them by an advisor model, and not in the
   field (addendum 2). In `design-01` four of the five come from one commit whose message says an advisor pass
   found them. So the checklists lean toward what such a reviewer notices. The same remark applies: it could
   only help Fable.
3. **The material is larger than the rules say:** about 93k and 84k tokens, against 20k to 70k (addendum 2).
4. **The advisor could be reached from harness runs until 17:36Z** (addendum 5). The owner's settings named
   Fable 5.2 as advisor, and a `claude -p` run can call a configured advisor although no tool list names it. The 36
   designer runs and the first 41 page builds ran in that state. None called it: a run that does reports a second
   model, and every stored attempt reports exactly one; an advisor call is billed as thousands of uncached input
   tokens, and every Fable designer run records 6. The settings named no advisor from 17:36Z, before the first
   checklist call and the first decision run; the same two checks hold for all 42 decision runs (at most 26
   uncached input tokens, one model each). Both checks were calibrated on one probe, so this is a strong inference
   and not a proof. The 1,167 runs stored in October also report one model each; they predate Fable 5.2, and
   whether a call would have shown the same way on October's versions of Claude Code was not re-tested. The 42
   decision runs did not have the harness's own off switch yet (it was added afterwards); they relied on the
   settings naming no advisor.
5. **Two batches of page builds were interrupted by the operator.** At 15:15Z, for the owner's pause, with four
   builders in flight; and at 17:52Z, when four builders had run for about 16 minutes (6 to 7 is normal) and had
   made no progress since about 17:40Z. An interrupted attempt is not logged, so nothing was counted, and the
   pages were built again. The four had started 41 to 105 seconds before the owner's settings file changed, and
   the link was unstable too (the watchdog, started at 17:52Z, logged a failed check at 17:54Z). The commit
   message of addendum 4 calls it a network interruption; addendum 5 says that was an inference, and the cause is
   not established. No log of either interruption exists. The builder is not a model under test.

## 10. Limits

- Two decision tasks and four briefs, all from one owner's projects. One of the two decision tasks separates
  nothing, so the decision row rests on one task.
- Twelve runs or twelve pairs show a large gap only. By chance alone, 9 or more of 12 blind preferences for one named
  side happen about one time in fourteen (one in seven for either side).
- Both judge models come from one vendor. The owner's blind choice is the only judgment from outside it, and it
  covers `ux-design` only.
- The instrument is a clean, read-only run with a neutral system prompt. A design agent inside a session has more
  context and tools.
- Only `fable-5-2@high` was tested as a designer, and no designer below `opus-5-5@xhigh`.
- The advisor measurement of section 7 used one task and at most five agents per arm. It counts calls; it does
  not measure whether a worker with an advisor does better work.

## 12. Follow-up the same night: a cheaper worker with an advisor (not audited)

**The pairing is not adopted: it was not cheaper than Opus alone, and it showed no gain.** Cell
`sonnet-5-5@xhigh+fable-advisor` on the three synthesis tasks, against the stored runs of `opus-5-5@xhigh` and
`sonnet-5-5@xhigh`. Planted items found, as the pinned judge counted them (both passes agreed in every run):

| Task (planted items) | Opus `xhigh` alone | Sonnet `xhigh` alone | Sonnet `xhigh` with a Fable advisor |
|---|---|---|---|
| `synthesize-01` (6) | 6, 6, 6 at $1.98 | 6, 6, 6 at $0.95 | 6, 6, 6 at $3.03; advisor called in 3 of 3 |
| `synthesize-02` (10) | 10, 10, 10 at $2.54 | 10, 10, 10 at $1.32 | 10, 10, 10 at $2.19; advisor called in 1 of 3 |
| `synthesize-03` (7) | 7, 7, 7 at $2.62 | 6, 6, 6 at $2.14 | 6, 6 at $2.15; advisor called in 0 of 2; one slot timed out twice |

- **Cost.** A run in which the advisor was called cost $3.19 on average, $2.00 of it the advisor's share (4
  runs). A run in which it was not called cost $1.80 (4 runs). Opus `xhigh` alone costs $2.38 on the same tasks.
  The harness's `decide` says of the cell: "not cheaper: $3.03 vs baseline $1.98" (the screening task).
- **Quality.** Where the advisor was called, Sonnet alone already finds every planted item. On `synthesize-03`,
  the one task where Sonnet alone misses an item that Opus finds, the advisor was not called in either valid run
  and the same count was missed. So the test shows no gain, and it cannot say what a call there would have done.
- **The model did not follow the instruction every time.** The cell's system prompt says to call the advisor once
  before the final answer. It was called in 4 of 8 valid runs.
- **Time.** The runs were slower than Sonnet alone (515 s against 344 s on the first task, 1,078 s against 822 s on
  the third, on average), and one slot of the third task hit the 1,200 s limit twice, with no failed connection
  check in either window.
- **A deviation:** the rule names 9 runs and 8 exist. The ninth slot's third attempt was not run, because no
  result of it could change the verdict: with a ninth run at no cost at all the mean would still be $2.22, above
  the bar of $1.79.
- **Spend:** $19.97 for the 8 valid runs and $5.12 for the judge; the two timed-out attempts have no recorded
  cost. This is apart from the round's $266.

By the rule nothing changes: the synthesis row stays `opus`/`xhigh`, and the table has no cell that depends on
