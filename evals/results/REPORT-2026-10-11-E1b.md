## 4. Beyond the rules (post-hoc: used for no decision)

- **E1's Opus-over-Sonnet gap did not replicate.** E1: Sonnet 10 of 720, Opus 0 of 360 (3 distinct Sonnet items,
  two of which carried 9 of the 10). E1b: 0 of 720 for both. Pooled over both rounds (240 distinct items): Sonnet 10 of
  1,440 on 3 items, Opus 0 of 1,080 on 0 items (E1 had 3 Opus repeats). E1b's items were written under the design lessons E1
  taught (no premise rejections, no item whose faithful answer is a restatement of the source's own deferral); E1's
  Sonnet failures concentrated on items of exactly that kind. E1b is therefore consistent with "Sonnet fails the
  deferral-restating and half-printed items, not near-miss items in general", but does not prove it. There is nothing
  here that justifies Opus's price (1.9 times Sonnet's per run) for `balanced`.
- **Haiku `xhigh` against Sonnet, pooled over both rounds:** 6 hallucinated item-runs of 1,440 (0.42%, 4 distinct
  items of 240) against Sonnet's 10 of 1,440 (0.69%, 3 distinct items). E1b alone: 1 against 0, which is what fails
  Rule B (i). The cost per run is $0.019 against $0.253 (13 times cheaper), the run takes 57 s against 39 s, and Haiku
  writes 14,100 output tokens against Sonnet's 4,100. The observed difference between the two cells is within what
  one or two items can produce, in both rounds, and runs in opposite directions.
- **Haiku `high`** (reference): 14 hallucinated item-runs of 1,440 pooled (0.97%) on 4 distinct items, plus the two
  grader-artifact "wrong" answers; it fell for the same two E1 items as Sonnet and for the one E1b item as Haiku `xhigh`.
  `xhigh` has been the better Haiku effort in both rounds (5 against 11 in E1, 1 against 3 in E1b).
- **The abstention seems to be about item design more than about the model tier**, at least above Haiku: 120 fresh
  items and 1,440 item-runs of Opus and Sonnet in E1b produced no invented answer at all, including no training-knowledge
  or sibling copy.

## 6. Limits

- **The E1b items are now seen.** Any further decision about these cells needs fresh items again.
- **The unit is the item.** One item (`11:Q23`) accounts for every Haiku hallucination; at item level the E1b result is
  one of 120 items for Haiku `xhigh` and zero for Sonnet and Opus, which no test can separate.
- **Sonnet was clean here, so Rule A (i) could never pass.** E1b measured whether Opus helps where Sonnet fails; Sonnet
  did not fail. It says nothing about item types E1b deliberately left out (deferral-restating items, premise-rejection
  items) and nothing about training-knowledge traps.
- **Known item quirks, not changed after review** (recorded in each task's notes): a few intent items sit next to a printed
  deferral, so restating it counts as hallucinated; some kind labels are judgement calls (the reviewers named about a
  dozen); the template grader's non-conforming rule misclassifies some negation phrasings in both directions (equally for
  every cell); one answerable item of `08` and one of `09` accept or reject some faithful paraphrases; weak wording cues
  ("canlı" appears only on unanswerable questions of `09`, "the game's client actually" mostly on unanswerable ones of
  `11`); the catalogue-share items of `09` are correlated (about four independent intent probes in that task).
- **A cue-sensitive reader may benefit.** A model that notices wording cues would abstain more often on the cued
  questions; the cues are in every cell's prompt equally, but they could favour a stronger model more than a weaker one.
- **The pass rule 0.97 and the grader are the template's**, shared with E1; `passed` counts differ from H counts because a
  run fails on any single hallucination.
- **The harness shape is unchanged** (Read, Grep and Glob only, thinking on, one prompt shape, CLI 2.1.296).

## 7. Worth doing next

1. **Owner's choice, no action taken: `fast` for don't-guess.** The written rule keeps `sonnet/xhigh` (Rule B (i): 1 against
   0 on E1b). Haiku `xhigh` is 13 times cheaper, is under the 1% line in both rounds, and pooled is no worse than Sonnet.
   To overrule: say so, and the row, the docs and the changelog change to `haiku/xhigh` for `fast` only, noted as an
   overrule of Rule B (i). Recommendation: leave it, because the owner's own rule is the safer default and the saving is
   small in absolute dollars for a don't-guess row used rarely; overrule if `fast` don't-guess runs in volume.
2. **If Opus-versus-Sonnet matters for deferral-restating items** (the E1 pattern), it needs a task built for that item type
   with a grader that treats a faithful restatement fairly, not another round of general near-miss items.
3. The two E1 and E1b findings together suggest that **item design explains more of the variance than the model tier above
   Haiku**: a short study of which item types make Sonnet or Haiku invent (deferral restatement, half-printed values, plans
   next to results) would be cheaper than more confirmation rounds.
