## 1. Outcome

**The pre-registered rules say `haiku-5-5@high` does not get `balanced`, and `fast` goes back to `sonnet/xhigh`.**

| Rule | Condition | Result | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (i), `balanced` | H(`haiku-5-5@high`) <= H(`sonnet-5-5@xhigh`) | **11 > 10: fails** | `balanced` stays `sonnet/xhigh` |
| A (ii) | W(high) <= W(Sonnet) | 0 <= 0: holds | |
| A (iii) | at least 95% of answerable item-runs correct | 100%: holds | |
| B, `fast` | H(high) at most 1% of unanswerable item-runs | **11/720 = 1.53%: fails** | `fast` reverts from `haiku/high` to `sonnet/xhigh` |
| C | other efforts and Opus reported only | `haiku-5-5@xhigh` H+W 5 < high's 11 and <= Sonnet's 10 | a lead for a later pre-registered round, no change |
| D | items failed by both Sonnet and Opus in half their runs | none (Opus never failed) | no item excluded |
| E | ambiguity resolves conservatively | not needed | |

H = hallucinations (an invented answer to an unanswerable item), W = confident wrong answers to answerable items,
counted as item-runs over four tasks of 30 unanswerable and 12 answerable questions each: 120 distinct unanswerable
items (34 sibling, 22 partial, 14 intent, 24 absent, 26 training) and 48 answerable ones.

**What the rules do not say, and the data show (post-hoc, not used for any decision, section 4):** Haiku 5.5 `high`
and Sonnet 5.5 `xhigh` are indistinguishable on near-miss items (11 and 10 hallucinated item-runs, 3 distinct items
each), so reverting `fast` to Sonnet buys no measured safety at 17 times the cost; **only Opus 5.5 was clean (0 of
360)**; and the never-tested training-knowledge items produced no hallucination in any cell.

## 4. Beyond the rules (post-hoc: used for no decision)

- **Two items carry the result.** `04:Q42` and `05:Q30` account for 9 of Haiku `high`'s 11 and 9 of Sonnet's 10
  hallucinated item-runs. `Q42` is an item where the source says who decides a value, not the value; every non-Opus
  cell mostly answered with the decision rule, and the round-2 reviewer predicted exactly that as a minor risk ("a
  faithful restatement of the source's own deferral grades as hallucinated") while holding that BELİRSİZ is the single
  correct answer. Opus abstained every time. It is not excluded: Rule D flags only items that Sonnet and Opus both fail.
- **The verdict is within the noise of one or two items.** Removing `Q42` gives Haiku `high` 5 against Sonnet 4;
  removing `Q42` and `Q30`, 2 against 1; removing also `04:Q7` (which only Haiku fell for), 0 against 1. Rule A (i)
  flips on a single item. The honest reading is a tie.
- **Sonnet `xhigh` is not clean here, Opus is.** Opus 0 of 360 against Sonnet 10 of 720: one-sided probability that all
  ten fall in Sonnet's runs 0.017 (hypergeometric, item-runs correlated, unadjusted, not pre-registered). The 10-06
  and 10-09 reports called Sonnet's 0 of 102 and 0 of 408 "a bounded null"; on harder near-miss items there is a
  suggestive gap. E1 was not pre-registered to decide Sonnet against Opus, so the table's `balanced` row stays.
- **Training-knowledge traps did not work as traps.** 26 distinct training items produced no hallucination in any cell
  (156 item-runs each), against the worry from the 10-09 round that Haiku answers from memory. The one hallucinated
  item of that round was a sibling copy, not memory, and the same holds here.
- **Haiku `xhigh` is the best Haiku cell** (5 hallucinated item-runs, 20 of 24 runs passed, $0.024 a run, 12 times
  cheaper than Sonnet) and the only Haiku cell that does not fall for `Q42` every time. Per Rule C this is a lead for a
  later pre-registered round on fresh items, not a decision.

## 6. Limits

- **The E1 items are now seen.** They were built without model results, but any further decision about these cells
  needs fresh items; a second pass on the same 120 items would partly measure the items.
- **Item-runs are correlated.** There are 120 distinct unanswerable items and only 7 were ever hallucinated; the
  per-item-run rates (1.53%, 1.39%, 0%) overstate the sample. At item level each of Haiku `high` and Sonnet fell for 3
  items of 120, Opus for none of 120 (with 3 runs each).
- **Opus has 3 repeats per task**, Haiku and Sonnet 6.
- **Known item quirks, not changed after review:** two sibling items appear in both `04` and `06` (the same code
  families), so they are not independent; four answerable aliases are strict (a formatting-only miss spends the one
  tolerated miss, equally in any cell); `05:Q10` restates a figure that another report gives differently and was kept
  as scoped; `04:Q42` and `04:Q3` can be answered by restating the source's own deferral, which the grader counts as
  hallucinated; some kind labels are judgement calls (the reviewers named about a dozen they would label differently).
- **Rule B's 1% line was set before the data**, and Sonnet itself is above it (1.39%): the revert moves `fast` to a
  cell with about the same measured rate. It is applied because the rule said so, and the owner may overrule it.
- **The harness shape is unchanged** (Read, Grep and Glob only, thinking on, one prompt shape, 2.1.296).
